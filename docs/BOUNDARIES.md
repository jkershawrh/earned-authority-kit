# Reference boundaries and remaining acceptance work

This kit demonstrates earned authority for a disposable model registry. It connects real canonical Passport, Foundation/PostgreSQL/OPA and Immutable Ledger services to pinned Cascade and deterministic GCL code. The target registry, independent readback connection, qualification history and local signed outbox use SQLite. This is a local reference implementation, not a production authorization system or a robot controller.

## What the observed runs establish

The current [fresh reconstruction report](../reports/observation-reference-combined.json) records 123 passing checks in a separate workspace on the same host: policy 8, service interfaces 7, lifecycle 23, fault/race 17, authority-operation recovery 41, retained-state restart 6 and historical operation evidence 21. Its seven child reports retain exact source hashes and observed results. The [service BDD report](../reports/service-bdd-live-010.json) records 52 passing executed cases and green acceptance for all 37 scenarios. `service-bdd-live-008.json` remains the earlier partial run. The `reference-*-011.json` reports re-run the same 123 checks on the existing stack with the current kit and retained volumes. A clean workspace is not an independently operated installation on a different machine. Earlier connected and clean-reference reports are retained as historical runs.

These runs exercise qualification, signed grants, current authority checks, bounded execution, independent registry readback, selective withdrawal, requalification, receipt verification and the specific injected faults named in the reports. Component tests additionally exercise signer/persistence failure, canonical field binding and concurrent mutations. They do not imply every possible fault was tested. The 37 local fixture BDD scenarios are not asserted to have all run against real services. Hardware robotics remains deferred.

## Local registry durability and authority audit are different boundaries

For a registry attempt, the local transaction records its qualification and authorization checks, intent event, registry mutation, mutation count, committed result and commit event together. The executor rechecks current signed authority while holding the shared authority fence. A retry of the same committed attempt reconciles its result rather than applying a second registry mutation. Independent observation occurs after the transaction; a crash can therefore leave a committed attempt whose outcome is still unknown. The recovery path must preserve that distinction. Ledger delivery occurs from the durable local outbox after the local transaction and does not turn an unavailable receipt into a known business failure.

The two per-attempt check records describe consumption of existing qualification and authority at the mutation boundary. They bind the exact current grant/passport, qualification revision and digest, signed command, and execution policy decision. Signed command fields must match the actual current authority; a valid signature alone cannot excuse a misattributed grant. Earlier qualification and issuance records retain their original identity and time. The consumer requires exact authenticated check event IDs, not just another record with the same attempt label. Legacy commands without the new check-version marker remain readable historical records and cannot borrow this increment's complete check coverage.

Explicitly refreshed authority observations provide durable outage diagnostics. Their cached ACTIVE status means active when observed; every envelope says it cannot authorize execution. Admission and commit still require the live canonical service, and a corrupt diagnostic cache does not affect those paths. Cache reads authenticate the local writer and exact agent/snapshot digest. Delayed replies cannot overwrite a newer authority revision or a later observation of the same revision. This cache is not an offline credential.

The requested-window contract uses a source-pinned demo blackout on 2100-01-01 from 00:00:00 through 00:01:00 UTC, excluding the end. It is an explicit test configuration, not a selected business calendar. Foundation/OPA denies overlapping proposed release intervals and release during the blackout using the engine clock. A future interval that passes policy cannot issue immediately usable authority: the coordinator requires the interval to be active, signs it into the grant, caps scope expiry, and rechecks it before dispatch and execution. No scheduler is implemented. Local enforcement trusts SystemClock; binding an OPA evaluation timestamp does not establish cross-host clock synchronization. See [the window contract](TIME_WINDOW_PROPOSAL.md).

Passport issuance/revocation and local lifecycle-event persistence remain separate commits. The coordinator now persists a signed authority-operation intent before dispatching a mutation. The intent binds the operation ID, agent, expected active passport and revision, complete requested change, target grant/qualification and intended lifecycle-event identity. Its durable states distinguish a prepared intent from a dispatched request whose response may be unknown. An unresolved intent blocks new registry execution and new authority mutations for that agent through cooperating coordinators sharing that registry database.

The additive canonical Apply/Get operation RPCs bind the complete normalized request to a durable signed receipt. Passport persists the receipt and authority transition in the same atomic state replacement. A retry with the same operation ID and request returns the original receipt without advancing the revision again; reusing the ID with different content is rejected. Authenticated historical lookup retrieves that receipt even after a later replacement, revocation, expiry or identity suspension. Recovery verifies its pinned signature and exact request, then records the lifecycle event and the journal's `APPLIED` state in one local transaction. A lost response can therefore be reconciled from evidence of the specific transition; an empty current aggregate is never treated as evidence that this coordinator performed the revocation.

A historical receipt proves a past transition, **not current permission**. Execution still obtains and verifies current canonical authority, checks the active revision, qualification and scope bounds, and holds the shared authority fence across the registry commit. Recovering a past `GRANTED` event does not restore a superseded or expired grant. Local events are delivered through the durable outbox; Passport, SQLite and Ledger still do not share one distributed transaction.

Cancellation also preserves uncertainty. A `PREPARED` intent that never crossed the dispatch marker may be cancelled if revalidation fails. Once `DISPATCHED`, an absent receipt or a failed-precondition response alone is insufficient: the same request may still be in flight, and that response can describe an inactive identity as well as a CAS conflict. Cancellation after refusal requires an observed canonical revision strictly greater than the request's expected revision, followed by another authenticated lookup that still finds no receipt. With persisted monotonic revisions, the old request can no longer commit. Unchanged, rolled-back, inconsistent or unavailable authority evidence leaves the intent unresolved rather than inventing failure or success.

The durable withdrawal queue targets a particular grant and qualification. If an old queued withdrawal resumes after a new grant replaces its target, it records `WITHDRAWAL_RECONCILED` with `TARGET_NOT_CURRENT` rather than revoking the new grant. For a dispatched authority operation, the immutable receipt supplies the missing transition evidence even if current authority has since changed; queue target checks and historical operation recovery serve different purposes.

[The operation component report](../evidence/canonical-passport-operations-result.json) records 44 Go tests / 76 leaf cases passing both normal and race checks, plus 24 Python Passport tests. It includes request binding, concurrent retry, signer/persistence failure, uncertain-commit recovery, historical lookup, provider/consumer vectors and V1 state migration. The retained reference state was also loaded offline without changing its state or signing key. These are component and migration checks, not a substitute for process-crash acceptance across the running services.

The [authority recovery run](../reports/observation-reference-v2-authority-recovery.json) passed 41 checks against actual services, using separate coordinator processes, explicit process exits, a forwarding proxy that drops a committed mutation response, and a real SQLite read-only write failure. ## Evidence admission and quarantine

Procedure self-reports are durable, signed claims kept separately from independent observations. They never add qualification passes, override registry readback, or create grants. A Foundation policy evaluation explicitly reports `executed: false` and `receipt_created: false`; an ALLOW response alone is not an executed registry action.

Receipt-backed evidence import checks the independently provisioned producer key and evaluator digest, the signed observation, exact attempt/procedure/context bindings, freshness, and the canonical Ledger receipt. The durable intake records the decision and retry history. A canonical receipt with an untrusted inner producer remains untrusted evidence. Authentication or integrity rejection produces an effective UNKNOWN, never a fabricated task FAIL. The same final retry decision creates no duplicate admission/rejection event; recovery from a verification outage must actually insert the admitted observation.

A submitted bad copy cannot revoke arbitrary authority. Explicit auditing examines the evidence references bound to the affected current grant. A positive integrity mismatch, missing required observation or stale evidence holds that qualification and queues withdrawal of that exact grant; an unrelated valid skill remains intact. Quarantine applies to the exact stored observation body. Qualification publication rechecks its references inside the local transaction, and grant and execution consumption recheck them again. An earlier eligible qualification cannot bypass a later quarantine of shared evidence. An audit also preserves a known failure recorded while remote verification was in flight.

Authentic local observations can remain usable while remote delivery is pending. A remote verification outage is recorded as unavailable and does not itself turn an observed PASS into a failure or withdraw otherwise valid local authority. Imported evidence needs its canonical receipt before admission. This distinction follows the local-durability policy D8; it is not a universal deployment policy.

The current trust roots and readback process are controlled by the same trusted host. Signatures establish provenance under those keys; they do not establish independent organizational control or protect against a malicious administrator. New matching requests can receive the exact evidence-hold reason only when an authenticated APPLIED revocation journal record matches the current canonical revision and active-passport identity, its prior target grant and request scope match, and the target qualification still has that HOLD reason. Old admitted commands remain fenced with `REVOKED_AUTHORITY`. A replacement grant takes precedence; a later unrelated authority revision conservatively falls back to generic revocation. Historical journal records never confer permission.

## Mutation authentication and uncertain retries

Every local event is signed against public verification keys pinned when the coordinator starts. The registry transaction reads back the newly persisted INTENT and verifies its exact row identity, attempt, payload and signature before changing the registry. Substituting another correctly signed event is insufficient. A failed writer signature or corrupted stored INTENT aborts the transaction before mutation.

The registry transaction distinguishes a body failure followed by a confirmed rollback from a failed commit acknowledgement. The former is a local durability denial; the latter requires reconciliation even if a best-effort rollback follows. SQLite, filesystem or local evidence-authentication failures after a known commit also cannot be presented as proof that execution was denied. The new UNKNOWN response preserves `committed: true` where commitment is established and uses null where it is not. It contains no invented outcome or receipt and does not overwrite the durable attempt.

`retry(command)` authenticates the command and first opens the attempt database read-only. A missing or inaccessible database cannot be recreated by that lookup. Missing or unreadable attempt records return a transient UNKNOWN response and cannot initiate a replacement attempt. A matching durable REQUESTED record may proceed through the ordinary fenced transaction; a concurrent or existing COMMITTED result is reconciled without a second mutation. Changed authenticated payloads remain conflicts.

[The standalone UNKNOWN contract](../contracts/unknown-reconciliation-result.md) describes response uncertainty, separate from a durable COMMITTED attempt whose independently observed outcome is UNKNOWN. These responses cannot enter the authenticated wire proof bundle. The component schedule also covers actual commit-then-error, readonly writes, missing files, wrong signing keys and stored-signature substitution. Arbitrary non-storage exceptions, every possible rollback/close failure and hostile host modification remain outside the named acceptance schedule. Authority-operation journal transactions retain their existing separate-service recovery behavior.

## Operating and trust assumptions

- Passport and the registry executor must share the **same operating-system advisory-lock domain**, the same fence file, and a filesystem with working `flock` semantics. On the validated macOS/Podman topology, Passport stays on the host with the executor; moving one into the Linux VM would break this assumption. All authority writers must acquire the fence before their service mutex. This is not a distributed lease or a multi-host revocation protocol.
- Host and container administrators, process configuration and installed trust roots remain trusted. Signatures do not prevent an administrator from replacing an issuer key, rolling back durable authority state, changing executable code, or bypassing the executor to write the registry directly. Anti-rollback anchoring, hardware key protection and hostile-operator resilience are separate work.
- OPA loads a frozen source and its binding data together. Its administrative network writes are denied, the mount is read-only, and file watching is disabled. Decisions carry the evaluated request and policy digest; the consumer pins that digest. The binding guarantee depends on that deployment discipline and trusted host administration.
- Host-facing HTTP and gRPC use loopback reference transport. Foundation uses a real local JWT/JWKS flow and gateway-to-BFF mTLS, but this does not establish secure remote access. Local key files and tokens, their rotation, backup, expiration and trust distribution are reference provisioning concerns, not a production secret-management design.
- Scope enforcement uses the earliest applicable signed scope, passport, embedded grant and qualification deadline. A parent passport may remain valid after an individual grant expires. Replacing an aggregate must not extend an unaffected grant's absolute bound; authority presence alone is insufficient to authorize it.
- The operation protocol assumes one canonical Passport writer process, durable state replacement and retained operation receipts. Receipt retention is currently unbounded; compaction, issuer-key rotation, downgrade from state V2 and multi-writer replication are not implemented. V1 state loads without a startup rewrite and upgrades on the next successful mutation; old transitions do not acquire fabricated historical receipts.
- Legacy Issue/Revoke clients remain compatible but do not create operation receipts. The coordinator's recovery guarantee requires its journal and the new operation RPCs. Other authenticated writers may change current authority; an unjournaled legacy change does not become part of this coordinator's exactly-once local lifecycle reconciliation.
- Cooperating coordinators serialize using a separate local journal lock and must share the registry database. The coordinator does not hold the Passport execution fence while awaiting a mutation RPC. This lock ordering prevents a coordinator from waiting for a service that needs a fence the coordinator itself holds; it does not coordinate unrelated databases or hosts.
- Restart checks cover retained local process/container state and volumes. They do not establish recovery from disk loss, filesystem corruption, backup rollback, cross-host failover, every legacy database migration, or high availability.

## Qualification and evaluation claims

The connected demo explicitly selects a **three-pass qualification profile**. It overrides Cascade's candidate and nano sample thresholds to three, requires accuracy 1 and zero false positives/negatives, and uses authenticated independent outcomes. The pinned canonical defaults instead require 50 samples for candidate and 200 for nano, with different accuracy/error thresholds; higher tiers have further requirements. Passing the demo profile does not demonstrate statistical adequacy or qualify a production procedure under those defaults.

GCL checks a declared, context-bound deployment-capacity proposal deterministically. It does not independently discover all real-world constraints, attest the business outcome, or issue authority. Ledger receipts authenticate recorded assertions; they do not prove that the assertions are true.

**EDD means evaluation-driven development. The planned EDD comparison has not run.** Held-out datasets, risk budget, sample-size adequacy, preregistered benefit thresholds and comparisons with fixed or human-approved policies remain acceptance work in [the evaluation protocol](../evaluations/README.md). There is no measured customer-value, operator-effort, economic advantage, robotics-safety, Red Hat support or production-readiness claim.

## Live recovery acceptance and remaining work

The recorded schedule covers durable intent and dispatch boundaries; canonical issue/revoke commits before local events; atomic local event/APPLIED persistence; selective and empty withdrawals; queue acknowledgement before deletion; later replacement without restoring old permission; actual lost replies; local persistence failure; and eventual receipt-verified delivery. The restart schedule rechecks previously issued receipts and local events rather than creating replacement authority.

Some adversarial details remain component-only: invalidating a prepared intent before dispatch, conservative cancellation after unchanged-revision precondition refusal, and cancellation after monotonic advancement with an absent receipt. Do not infer live coverage for those branches from the 41-check total. Read the exact named checks and fault mechanism in each report before expanding an acceptance claim.

Key/state backup restoration, revision rollback resistance, receipt-retention policy, key rotation and cross-host failure recovery remain separate follow-on design and acceptance work. A successful ordinary restart does not establish rollback protection. The full real-service 37-scenario BDD corpus and evaluation-driven comparison also remain unasserted unless separately recorded.
